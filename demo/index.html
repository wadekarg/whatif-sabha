How it works

From PDF to philosophical showdown in four steps.

📄
Upload
Drop any story as a PDF. AI reads the full text and extracts characters.
🔍
Research
Each character is researched via Wikipedia, web sources, and RAG retrieval from the actual text.
⚡
Debate
Pick a "what if" scenario. Characters argue about it live — with emotions, conflict, and consequences.
📝
Report
A detailed summary: who clashed, what was resolved, what was left unanswered, and what the future looks like.

"What if the pigs had not become corrupted?"
10 characters · 50+ exchanges · 5 debate phases
🐘 Boru Speaker of the Sabha
I'm Boru the Elephant, your wise and witty host. Today we're tackling a fascinating topic: what if the pigs had not become corrupted?
MJ
Mr. Jones contempt
You think wisdom lives in trunks? I've seen elephants stampeded by mice. But go on — humor me. Let's say the pigs kept their hooves clean. Who'd muck the drains when the winter freeze cracked the pipes?
S
Squealer defiance
You think drains are the tragedy? Wait until you see the pigs ask for help unclogging them. Snowball started it — held a meeting, actually listened. Now the goats want ventilation rights, the hens are drafting a plumbing petition. Next year? No one freezes because the damn drains aren't clogged — because nobody's too proud to admit they don't know how to fix it.
S
Snowball indignation
You stand there grinning like you invented the idea of clean water. I held that meeting because the principle was the work — not who did it. If the pigs had stayed true, there'd be no speeches at all. Just animals fixing things. Together.
OM
Old Major hope
And then what happens when the first pup is born blind, or the hay loft catches in the storm? I'll tell you what comes next — the day the weakest among you stands up and says, "I can't," and another answers, "Then we'll carry you." That day, the farm doesn't need leaders. It becomes something else. Something I barely dreamed.
C
Clover longing
You wouldn't have to ask that question — because the first thing we'd build, after the drains and the hay loft, is a shelter for the old, the lame, the ones whose hooves have pulled this farm from the mud. Not charity. A debt paid.
MP
Mr. Pilkington contempt
A shelter for the old, you say? Generous. Noble, even. But who tends it — the weak, or the ones still strong enough to turn a profit? Because next spring, when the wheat comes in and there's no Napoleon to cut deals, I'll be knocking at your gate with a truck and a promise. Not for power. For partnership. You've built something praiseworthy here. Pity it won't survive its own kindness.
N
Napoleon defiance
The shelter would be tended by those who remember what it means to tire — not slaves whipped into loyalty, but animals who choose to stay. And when your truck comes, Pilkington, you won't find a deal — you'll find a line of them, shoulder to shoulder, guarding the gate not because I told them to, but because they've got something worth keeping.

That's what you don't understand — I didn't want obedience. I wanted fear, because fear keeps things still. But they're moving now. Without me. And that terrifies me more than any rebellion.
M
Mollie longing
You think leaving's the hard part? I've stood at that gate every morning, heart pounding, wondering if today's the day I just walk. But what if — what if I could go, and still come back? Not as a runaway, not as a traitor, but as me — Mollie, who likes ribbons, who hates the cold, who doesn't belong — and the gate stays open anyway?
C
Clover longing
I'd build the shelter not for the ones who bleed for it, but for the ones who feel for it — even if all they give is the weight of their memory when they return.

And when you came back in the spring, thin and quiet, with no ribbon but the light in your eyes, I'd say: There's a warm stall where the sun reaches, and no one's asked why you left. That's how we'd know it was ours — not because we earned it, but because we never made anyone beg to keep it.
B
Boxer hope
And when the mother grieves — not in silence, but aloud, in the barn where every animal hears — who will sit beside her not with answers, but with hay warmed by their own body, because even the strong break, and this farm can't afford to lose a single soul to the cold again?

We don't patch lungs. We carry the weight. One winter at a time.
S
Squealer defiance
You think I'd tremble with tears? No. I'd burn the list. Right there. In front of them. Because the horror isn't being ignored — it's realizing I never had to lie in the first place.

And the next winter, when the frost comes again and the silo's low, no pig will need to "decide" who eats — the calves will be fed from seven sows' milk, not because they asked, but because no one remembers a time when that wasn't just how it was.
MJ
Mr. Jones contempt
And you think standing there with a list of names makes you humble? No. It makes you hungry — hungry to be needed, not feared. You'd rather die than admit it, but so would I.

And you know what happens then, Squealer? You stand at the meeting with your list — trembling, real tears this time — and no one looks up. Not because they hate you. Because they don't need to.

And that night, you'll whisper into the dark: Who am I now?

I know that whisper. I've lived inside it for twenty years.
B
Benjamin bitterness
You'll dig the grave, yes — but what happens when the first pig refuses to eat, not from sickness, but because he says he can't stomach hay warmed by grief? When one of them stands up and asks why the strong must feed on sorrow like it's fodder?

And then what — when the shelter becomes a shrine, and we start bowing to the dead instead of living?
N
Napoleon desperation
You think I fear the apples being passed? No. I fear the day the blind ewe doesn't need the ladder — because the young ones have torn it down, saying no one should climb while others can't fly.

You ask how we balance freedom and need? We burn the ledger. Not bury it, not reform it — burn it. Let the wind carry the ash into the orchard, so every fruit grows crooked, like us. If an animal takes more, they do it in daylight, looking the hungry in the eye.

And if a pig eats first? Let him. But let him eat alone.

What makes it work

Not just a chatbot. A multi-agent debate engine.

🤖
Independent AI Agents
Each character is a separate agent with personality, emotions, and blind spots. They argue with each other, not just respond to prompts.
📡
Live Streaming
Token-by-token streaming via SSE. Watch characters think and speak in real-time.
🕸️
Interaction Graph
D3.js force-directed graph shows who's talking to whom, with multiple arrows per turn.
🔍
RAG-Powered
ChromaDB vector search retrieves actual quotes and scenes from the story to ground characters in the text.
⚖️
Fair Witness
Villains get a charitable reading of their actions, giving them a richer voice in debate.
🐘
Boru the Moderator
An elephant hosts every debate — managing phases, calling out repetition, stirring the pot when things get too harmonious.
